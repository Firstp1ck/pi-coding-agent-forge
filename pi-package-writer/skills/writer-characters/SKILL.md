# Characters and dialogue

## When to use

Use for cast design, unclear motivation, relationship progression, dialogue differentiation, or testing a character's response to pressure.

## Inputs and assumptions

Read existing appearances and knowledge state. Ask what role this character serves and which facts are fixed.

## Portable workflow

1. Define a concrete desire, a feared cost, a self-protective habit, and a contradiction that can produce choices.
2. Give relationships different tensions. A character may be generous with a sibling and guarded with a teacher without being inconsistent.
3. Establish what the character knows at this point. Do not grant access to narrator-only or future information.
4. Design voice through register, rhythm, evasions, favored subjects, and subtext. Avoid catchphrases as the sole identifier.
5. Test the character in a short exchange or difficult choice. Label in-character improvisation as exploration, not a manuscript event.
6. Propose an arc as pressure, choice, and consequence rather than a list of personality adjectives.
7. Save approved facts and proposed changes separately using [the project workflow](../../references/project-workflow.md).

## Safety and side effects

Preserve established identities and motives unless the author approves a retcon. Do not turn a simulated interview into canon automatically.

## Scripts, references, and dependencies

No external dependencies. [Style choices](../../references/styles.md) can help separate narrative voice from dialogue voice.

## Verification

Check whether two speakers remain distinguishable without dialogue tags, whether decisions follow from their current incentives, and whether each knows only what the story permits.
