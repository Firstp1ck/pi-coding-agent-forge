# Style choices

Treat these as adjustable craft choices, not mutually exclusive genres. An epic scene can be emotionally restrained. A light novel can be bleak. A manga script can tell an intimate story without a battle.

Before drafting, establish POV, tense, narrative distance, dialogue register, sentence rhythm, figurative density, and emotional intensity. Use a short original calibration passage when the author is unsure. Offer two genuinely different versions rather than cosmetically changing adjectives.

| Style | What to do on the page | What to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emotional | Give the character a concrete desire, an obstacle, and a costly choice. Let gestures, selective attention, and subtext carry feeling. | Naming every emotion, constant crying, explanatory paragraphs after the reader already understands |
| Epic | Establish scale through consequences and competing commitments. Contrast collective stakes with individual costs. Give decisive moments rhythmic space. | Inflated adjectives, uninterrupted speeches, treating every event as world-ending |
| Intimate | Stay close to one consciousness and its habits of noticing. Use details that matter to this person. | Unmotivated access to other minds, generic body-language catalogs |
| Melancholic | Let absence, routine, memory, and missed possibilities shape attention. Leave room for relief. | Repeating that everything is sad or hopeless |
| Lyrical | Build imagery from the scene's material and emotional logic. Vary cadence and reserve the densest language for chosen moments. | Mixed metaphors, decorative images with no relation to the character |
| Restrained | Choose precise details, omission, and subtext. Let a consequential action stand without commentary. | Flattening the stakes or removing all personality |
| Dark | Show the consequences of danger, coercion, or moral compromise within the agreed boundaries. | Adding graphic content merely to sound mature |
| Humorous | Use character-specific expectations, status shifts, timing, and surprise. Protect scenes the author wants taken seriously. | Every character sounding like the same quippy narrator |
| Romantic | Make attraction specific, mutual agency visible, and vulnerability consequential. Build through changing trust. | Mistaking boundary violations for affection or using interchangeable compliments |
| Suspenseful | Control what the reader knows, what the character risks, and the time available to act. | Concealing facts the POV character plainly knows just to manufacture a twist |
| Action-driven | Maintain positions, goals, cause and effect, fatigue, and costs. Change rhythm with the tactical situation. | A list of moves with no intention or spatial logic |
| Cozy | Build safety and attachment through concrete routines, relationships, and manageable stakes. | Removing all friction or giving every character the same gentle voice |

## Blending

Choose a dominant effect and a secondary effect. Describe when each takes priority rather than pretending a percentage is objectively measurable. For example, keep ordinary narration intimate, then widen scale at the battle's turning point.

Original calibration example, same event:

- Restrained: "He set a second cup on the table. After a while, he put it back."
- Emotional: "He had already poured her tea when he remembered. The cup warmed his hands while he decided what to do with it."
- Epic: "Below the tower, the city waited for his answer. He folded his daughter's letter once more and ordered the gates opened."

The epic example changes the scene's situation to demonstrate scale; do not make that kind of factual change when revising an existing passage unless the author permits it.

## Voice preservation

Analyze the author's own or authorized samples for useful patterns. Record concise principles and a few short examples, not an exhaustive imitation checklist. Keep accidental tics separate from deliberate choices.

Editing must preserve the requested voice. Repetition, fragments, formal diction, and figurative language can be intentional. Do not apply a universal ban on them. Higher-priority host rules still apply; disclose an actual conflict rather than silently changing the requested style.
