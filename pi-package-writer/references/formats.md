# Writing formats

Format does not determine genre, audience, language, or emotional tone. Confirm those separately. "Roman" means a novel here, not necessarily a romance.

## Novel

Plan the central dramatic question, character change, major consequences, and ending promise. Choose a structure that fits the story. Three-act structure, episodic structure, braided timelines, and kishotenketsu are options, not mandatory templates. Chapters should change the situation, knowledge, relationships, or reader expectations.

## Short story

Limit the cast and competing promises to what the chosen length can resolve or deliberately leave open. Begin near a meaningful pressure point. Do not compress an entire novel into a summary of events. An ending may change the reader's understanding rather than provide a large twist.

## Light novel

Ask for audience, language, intended volume length, and desired relationship between prose and illustrations. Use a distinct narrative voice and readable scene progression. Internal monologue, dialogue, and illustrations are available devices, not mandatory quotas. Do not assume isekai, school settings, harem conventions, Japanese honorifics, or simplistic language.

Give a volume a satisfying local arc while leaving deliberate series questions open. Illustration notes should identify useful visual moments and sit outside manuscript prose.

## Web novel and serial fiction

Plan installments with a local reader reward and a continuing promise. Track release units, recaps, open threads, and knowledge changes. Use cliffhangers when earned; an emotional decision, revelation, reversal, or satisfying partial resolution can also carry the reader forward.

Avoid filler, repetitive power escalations, and recaps that exist only to reach a target length. Adapt to a named platform only when the author asks. Market research must not become unapproved scraping or manuscript upload.

## Manga

Confirm page budget, reading direction, intended audience, and whether the output is a script or thumbnail plan. Plan visible actions, expressions, dialogue, captions, sound effects, and silent beats. Reserve page-turn reveals intentionally. A panel shows a drawable instant, not five sequential actions.

Use a practical script shape:

```text
Page 1, right-to-left reading
Purpose: Establish the empty station and the character's hesitation.
Panel 1, wide: Empty platform. One lit vending machine at the far end.
  Caption: None.
  Sound: Fluorescent hum.
Panel 2, close: Her thumb pauses over an unanswered message.
  Dialogue: None.
Panel 3, medium: She puts the phone away as the train arrives.
  Sound: Brakes.
Page turn: Reveal who is waiting inside the train.
```

Do not cram prose narration into speech bubbles. Preserve interiority by choosing expressions, actions, visual motifs, or sparse captions. Ask before changing a source event to make it drawable.

## Webtoon

Design for vertical scrolling rather than printed page turns. Use spacing, panel sequence, reveal distance, and phone-screen readability. Mark episode boundaries and scroll beats. Do not describe a scroll reveal as a two-page spread.

## Visual production boundary

This package writes text scripts, storyboards, character descriptions, and optional image-prompt drafts. It does not draw pages or call image services. A separate, explicitly authorized image workflow must handle rendering, costs, asset consistency, and review.
